How the severity engine works

When an inspector submits a finding, ConstructFlow reads the description and recommends a severity: Low, Moderate, High, or Critical. It is a rule-based expert system: every result comes from a word in its vocabulary and one cell of the severity grid below, so it can always be explained. The supervisor makes the final decision and can keep or change the recommendation.

How it decides

The same five steps run for every report, in English, Tagalog, or Taglish.

  1. 1Reads the words. Capital letters and filler words such as the, ang, and sa are ignored, and word endings are trimmed, so cracks, cracking, and cracked all count as crack.
  2. 2Finds the defect and how serious it is (D, 1 to 4). A defect word names the type (crack, bitak, honeycombing, tagas). A condition word sets the level, for example hairline = 1 and wide = 4. If no condition is described, the type's usual level (2) is used.
  3. 3Finds the building part and how serious its failure would be (C, 1 to 4). A column, beam, or slab is 4; paint is 1. If no part is named, it uses finishes affecting use (2) and never assumes a load-bearing part; the one exception is settlement, which is the foundation itself moving, so it is read as the footing (4). Words about people at risk (workers below, residents) raise C by one.
  4. 4Reads words that change the meaning. No, walang, or hindi cancels what follows it in the same clause (no cracks, but spalling on the beam keeps only the spalling). Words like severe or malaki raise D by one; minor or maliit lower it by one.
  5. 5Reads the severity from the grid. The defect level (D) and the consequence level (C) point to one cell. The two numbers are never multiplied.

A report is flagged for the supervisor's review when:

  • no listed defect type is recognized, or no building part is named;
  • a defect word was matched through a misspelling;
  • a second defect type, or a more serious building part, is also mentioned;
  • a measurement such as 0.2 mm is given (the engine does not grade measurements);
  • the inspector's own conclusion (stop work, for NCR) is two or more levels away from the result.

Writing a finding that classifies well

  • Name the building part: crack on the column, not just crack.
  • Describe the condition: hairline, deep, wide, continuous, with rust, rebar visible.
  • One finding per report. If you see two different problems, file two reports.
  • Mention people at risk when there are any: workers below, residents, pedestrians.
  • Measurements are welcome but are checked by the supervisor; the engine flags them instead of grading them.
  • English, Tagalog, or Taglish are all fine. If a word is not in the vocabulary, the report is flagged so the supervisor decides.
