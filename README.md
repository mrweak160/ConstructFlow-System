ConstructFlow 
Rule Based AI Severity Recommendation and Work Order Coordination System for Small Construction Teams 

Small construction teams rarely lose track of a problem because nobody noticed it. They lose track because the notice lived in a group chat, a camera roll, and somebody's memory of what the foreman said on Tuesday.

ConstructFlow is built around one idea: a finding isn't useful until it has a severity, an owner and a deadline attached to it. Every site issue recorded in the system becomes a work order somebody is accountable for, and the most dangerous items rise to the top on their own.

A cracked load-bearing column and a scuffed handrail are both "damage," and in a group chat they look identical. ConstructFlow separates them at the point of reporting, and keeps them separated — the queue a field worker sees is ordered by how serious the job is, not by when it was filed.

Who uses it

Administrator — the business owner. Sets up the team, brings people on by email, and decides what each person does.

Supervisor — runs the work. Sends inspectors out to look at things, turns what they find into assigned jobs, and tracks what's open, stalled or done.

Field Inspector — walks the site. Records what they find with photos and location, and rates how serious it is.

Field Worker — does the work. Sees only what's assigned to them, most urgent first, and reports progress from their phone.

How work moves

A supervisor sends an inspector to check something. The inspector walks the site and files what they find, with photos, the location, and a severity rating. The supervisor turns that finding into a job, assigns it to a worker, and sets a deadline. The worker does it and reports back. The supervisor closes it.

Every step points back at the one before. A finished job can be traced to the inspection that caused it, and an inspection can be traced to the person who asked for it. Nothing goes missing between the site and the office.

Getting people in

There's no public sign-up. The business owner registers and creates the team, then adds everyone else by email — entering their name, role and details, and sending them a link to set their own password.

That keeps the roster deliberate. Nobody joins a construction team by finding a website; somebody who already works there puts them on it.
